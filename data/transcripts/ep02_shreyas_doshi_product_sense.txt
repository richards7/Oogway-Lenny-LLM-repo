# Episode Title: Shreyas Doshi on Product Sense, Pre-Mortems, LNO Framework, and High-Agency PMing

Lenny: Welcome back to Lenny's Podcast. Today my guest is Shreyas Doshi, former product leader at Stripe, Twitter, Google, and Yahoo. Shreyas, welcome!

Shreyas Doshi: Thank you Lenny, great to be here.

Lenny: Shreyas, you're famous for defining 'Product Sense'. How do you define product sense, and can it be learned or cultivated?

Shreyas Doshi: Product sense is empathy for user problems combined with domain intuition, allowing a product manager to make sound decisions under conditions of high ambiguity without complete data. Many people think product sense is innate, but it is actually a trainable discipline. You cultivate product sense by:
1. Deeply observing real user behaviors and pain points rather than relying solely on user feedback surveys.
2. Analyzing successful products in non-competing domains to understand why their UX choices work.
3. Conducting rigorous post-mortems and pre-mortems on your own product decisions to calibrate your intuition over time.

Lenny: Tell us about your famous LNO Framework for time management and prioritization.

Shreyas Doshi: Most PMs burn out because they treat every task with equal perfectionism. The LNO Framework categorizes tasks into three buckets:
- L Tasks (Leverage Tasks): High-impact work where 10x effort yields 100x results (e.g., product strategy, core architecture decisions, key hire interviews). You must do these with extreme excellence (100% effort).
- N Tasks (Neutral Tasks): Necessary operational work that must be done well enough, but extra perfection adds no value (e.g., weekly status reporting, sprint grooming). Aim for 80% quality.
- O Tasks (Overhead Tasks): Administrative tasks that just need to get done (e.g., expense reports, basic email replies). Spend minimum viable effort (50% quality) or delegate/automate.

Lenny: What is the Pre-Mortem technique, and how should product teams run one before launching a major initiative?

Shreyas Doshi: A pre-mortem takes place BEFORE a project starts. Gather your team and say: 'Imagine we are 12 months in the future, and this launch was a complete, catastrophic failure. Write down 5 specific reasons why we failed.'
By giving team members permission to voice doubts upfront without being seen as unsupportive, you uncover hidden risks, flawed assumptions, and critical dependencies that traditional risk logs completely miss.

Lenny: How can PMs build high agency in their careers?

Shreyas Doshi: High agency is the determination to find a way to make things happen, regardless of constraints, bureaucracy, or lack of resources. Low-agency PMs make excuses about team capacity or lack of clear directions; high-agency PMs reframe the problem, find creative workarounds, and align cross-functional peers around a shared vision.
